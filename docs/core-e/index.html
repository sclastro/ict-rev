適用：2025 年起香港中學文憑考試資訊及通訊科技科（新課程）

依據：課程發展議會與考評局《資訊及通訊科技課程及評估指引》、考評局 2023 至 2025 年考試簡報會資料、2025 年樣本試卷、2012 年練習卷、歷屆試題及評卷參考、香港電腦教育學會 2025 及 2026 年模擬試卷

單元 E 是必修部分課時最少的單元，介紹新興科技的概念和應用、使用資訊及通訊科技對健康的影響、公平存取和道德問題，以及知識產權。分為三個課題：

課題 課時 對應課本章節
a. 科技創新 3 第 1 章
b. 健康及道德議題 3 第 2 章
c. 知識產權 2 第 3 章

單元 E 的甲部題目多放在卷末，每年約有兩至四題；乙部則常與其他單元合為一題，例如在網站設計的題目中問怎樣保護版權，或在電腦使用的題目中問健康問題。這類題目字面淺易，但失分往往因為答得籠統：寫健康問題要寫明受影響的身體部位，寫保護措施要寫具體做法。

考前一頁清單

科技創新

  • 模式識別：分析輸入的數據（文字、圖像、聲音），找出模式並分類
  • 機器學習的流程：收集數據 → 清理及整理數據 → 訓練模型 → 測試模型
  • 監督式學習用有標籤的數據；非監督式學習用沒有標籤的數據；半監督式學習兩者結合；強化式學習按回饋（獎勵或懲罰）改進
  • 訓練數據要數量多、包含不同變化，並包括正例和反例（例如有火和沒有火的相片）
  • 大數據 5V：數量、速度、多樣、真實性、價值
  • 3D 打印是逐層添加材料的增材製造；FDM 成本最低、最安全；SLA 表面光滑、細節精細；SLS 用粉末，毋須支撐結構
  • AR 把數碼資訊疊加在真實環境上；VR 令用戶完全沉浸在虛擬世界；MR 讓真實和虛擬物件互動

健康及道德議題

  • 電腦視覺綜合症：眼睛乾澀、疲勞、視力模糊、頭痛；預防：20-20-20 法則、防眩光屏幕、充足照明、多眨眼
  • 重複性勞損影響手腕、手指；預防：人體工程學鍵盤和滑鼠、腕托、定時休息
  • 良好姿勢：屏幕距離 50 至 70 厘米，屏幕中心低於視線約 20°；手肘約成 90°；雙腳平放地面或腳踏上；椅子可調高度和靠背
  • 方便用戶使用的界面：簡單清晰、一致、無障礙
  • 網上資訊自由的好處：資訊更新快、種類多、大多免費；壞處：未經審查、來源不可靠、容易沉迷
  • 數碼隔閡的成因：收入、年齡、性別、教育程度、殘疾、種族及語言、地理位置
  • 收窄數碼隔閡：提供設備和上網服務，並教授使用技能；只提供網上課程不能收窄數碼隔閡
  • 道德議題：私隱（DNA、容貌識別）、環境（電子廢物、耗電）、社交媒體（網絡欺凌、起底、假新聞）

知識產權

  • 版權在作品完成時自動產生，毋須註冊；商標、專利、外觀設計須註冊
  • 版權不保護概念、事實、數據、名稱
  • 共享軟件：先試後買；免費軟件：免費使用，不提供源碼；開放源碼軟件：可修改及重新分發源碼；公用軟件：不受版權限制
  • 商業軟件的優點：有技術支援；開放源碼軟件的優點：免費、可修改、可透過協作持續改進
  • 保護數碼作品：數碼水印、數碼簽署、版權聲明、加密或限制存取、停用下載及截圖功能
  • 香港海關負責執法；知識產權署負責註冊商標、專利及外觀設計，並推廣保護知識產權
  • 侵權可遭民事索償，嚴重者屬刑事罪行，可被罰款及監禁
  • 引用資料要註明出處；使用人工智能工具也要註明

Module E has the fewest teaching hours in the compulsory part. It covers the concepts and applications of emerging technologies, the effects of using ICT on health, equity of access and ethical issues, and intellectual property. It has three topics:

Topic Hours Textbook chapters
a. Technological Innovations 3 Ch. 1
b. Health and Ethical Issues 3 Ch. 2
c. Intellectual Property 2 Ch. 3

Module E questions in Section A usually appear at the end of the paper, about two to four each year; in Section B they are often combined with other modules, for example asking how to protect copyright in a question on website design, or about health problems in a question on computer use. These questions look easy, but marks are often lost through vague answers: when stating a health problem, name the part of the body affected; when stating a protective measure, describe exactly what to do.

One-page checklist before the exam

Technological innovations

  • Pattern recognition: analyse input data (text, images, sound), find patterns and classify them
  • Machine learning process: collect data → clean and prepare data → train the model → test the model
  • Supervised learning uses labelled data; unsupervised learning uses unlabelled data; semi-supervised learning combines both; reinforcement learning improves through feedback (rewards or penalties)
  • Training data should be large in number, include different variations, and include positive and negative examples (e.g. photos with and without fire)
  • The 5Vs of big data: volume, velocity, variety, veracity, value
  • 3D printing is additive manufacturing that adds material layer by layer; FDM is the cheapest and safest; SLA gives smooth surfaces and fine details; SLS uses powder and needs no support structures
  • AR overlays digital information on the real environment; VR immerses users completely in a virtual world; MR lets real and virtual objects interact

Health and ethical issues

  • Computer vision syndrome: dry, tired eyes, blurred vision, headaches; prevention: the 20-20-20 rule, anti-glare screens, adequate lighting, blinking more
  • Repetitive strain injury affects the wrists and fingers; prevention: ergonomic keyboards and mice, wrist rests, regular breaks
  • Good posture: screen 50–70 cm away, with its centre about 20° below eye level; elbows at about 90°; feet flat on the floor or a footrest; chair with adjustable height and backrest
  • User-friendly interfaces: simple and clear, consistent, accessible
  • Pros of freedom of information on the Internet: up-to-date, varied, mostly free; cons: unverified, unreliable sources, addictive
  • Causes of the digital divide: income, age, gender, education level, disability, ethnicity and language, geographical location
  • Bridging the digital divide: provide devices and Internet access, and teach the skills to use them; providing online lessons alone does not bridge the digital divide
  • Ethical issues: privacy (DNA, facial recognition), environment (e-waste, energy use), social media (cyberbullying, doxxing, fake news)

Intellectual property

  • Copyright arises automatically when a work is created, with no registration; trade marks, patents and designs must be registered
  • Copyright does not protect ideas, facts, data or names
  • Shareware: try before you buy; freeware: free to use, no source code; open source software: source code may be modified and redistributed; public domain software: no copyright restrictions
  • Advantage of commercial software: technical support; advantages of open source software: free, modifiable, continuously improved through collaboration
  • Protecting digital works: digital watermarks, digital signatures, copyright notices, encryption or access control, disabling download and screenshot functions
  • The Customs and Excise Department enforces the law; the Intellectual Property Department registers trade marks, patents and designs and promotes IP protection
  • Infringers may face civil claims; serious cases are criminal offences punishable by fines and imprisonment
  • Acknowledge the sources you cite; acknowledge the use of AI tools too
