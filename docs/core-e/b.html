健康及道德議題Health and Ethical Issues

Health and Ethical Issues健康及道德議題
建議課時 3 小時Suggested time: 3 hours課本第 2 章Textbook Ch. 2

2.1課程要求

辨別使用資訊及通訊科技對健康造成的威脅，並建議實踐良好的人機工程學（包括減少重複性勞損、辦公室家具的適當設計及擺設、軟件的設計如容易使用和具一致性的用戶界面，以及工作環境）；認識公平存取的重要性，陳述互聯網上資訊自由的好處和壞處，並從數碼隔閡、性別平等和殘障人士獲得機會等方面，以及從地區和全球角度，了解相關的公平問題；討論使用資訊及通訊科技時的道德考慮因素。

2.2使用資訊及通訊科技的健康威脅

健康問題 症狀 成因 預防方法
電腦視覺綜合症（CVS） 眼睛乾澀、疲勞、刺痛，視力模糊，對眩光敏感，頭痛 長時間近距離注視屏幕、眨眼次數減少、屏幕反光或眩光、照明不足 遵從 20-20-20 法則；使用防眩光屏幕；在光線充足的環境使用；多眨眼；限制使用屏幕的時間
背部及頸部疼痛 腰背、頸、肩疼痛 坐姿不良、長時間維持同一姿勢、低頭看手機 保持正確坐姿；屏幕調至合適高度；每 15 至 20 分鐘伸展一下
深層靜脈血栓 腿部腫痛 長時間坐着，腿部缺乏活動，血液滯留在腿部靜脈 定時站立走動、伸展雙腿
重複性勞損（RSI） 手腕、手指、手肘疼痛、腫脹，活動能力下降 長時間重複同一動作，例如打字、使用滑鼠、滑動手機屏幕 使用人體工程學鍵盤和滑鼠、腕托；定時休息並伸展手部
上網成癮 焦慮、抑鬱、失眠、社交孤立、忽略學業和責任 無法控制地長時間上網、玩電子遊戲、使用社交媒體 記錄並限制上網時間；多參與運動和群體活動；有需要時尋求社工或輔導人員協助

20-20-20 法則：每使用屏幕 20 分鐘，望向最少 20 英尺（約 6 米）外的景物 20 秒。

⚠ 電腦視覺綜合症源於長時間注視屏幕，不是由顯示器發出的電磁輻射造成。答題時寫「長時間注視屏幕」「眨眼減少」「屏幕眩光」。

〔改編自 2022 卷一甲第 37 題〕經常沉迷瀏覽社交媒體和觀看網上影片，可能引致眼睛不適和視力模糊，以及與家人朋友疏離；不會令體能改善。

2.3良好的人體工程學

人體工程學研究怎樣設計設備、家具、軟件和工作環境，令使用者舒適、安全，減少受傷。

正確的姿勢和擺設

項目 建議
屏幕 與眼睛相距約 50 至 70 厘米；屏幕頂部約與視線同高，屏幕中心低於視線約 20°；屏幕角度可調，避免反光
鍵盤和滑鼠 放在手肘高度；手肘約成 90°，前臂與手掌保持平直
椅子 高度和靠背可調；有承托腰背的靠背；可調扶手承托前臂；五腳底座較穩固
雙腳 平放在地面；雙腳碰不到地面時用腳踏
桌子 高度合適，最好可調；有足夠空間擺放鍵盤和滑鼠
工作環境 照明充足，避免燈光或窗外光線直射屏幕；通風良好

人體工程學設備：人體工程學鍵盤（讓雙手保持自然位置）、人體工程學滑鼠、凝膠腕托、可調高度的顯示器支架、防眩光屏幕、可調校的椅子和桌子、腳踏。

⚠ 〔2024 卷一乙 2(d)〕湯姆的工作需要長時間使用鍵盤。寫出一項潛在的健康威脅，以及一項相應的保護措施（定時休息除外）。

✔ 健康威脅：手腕的重複性勞損、腕管綜合症，或手腕、手掌、手指疼痛。措施：使用人體工程學設計的鍵盤，讓雙手保持自然位置；或使用矽膠腕托。

✘ 只寫「頭痛」或「疼痛」而沒有寫明身體部位，不給分；寫「停止工作一會兒」不接受，因為題目已排除定時休息。

〔2024 卷一甲第 39 題〕按題目圖中的電腦使用情況，應作的人體工程學改善是在椅子上加裝扶手，而不是加裝軌跡球、觸控屏幕或改用較小的桌子。93% 考生答對。

〔樣本試卷卷一甲第 40 題〕良好的人體工程學設定中，顯示器的角度和桌子的高度應可調校；扶手的角度則不是必需的。

〔2012 練習卷卷一甲第 38 題〕扶手有助減少打字時手指和手腕的重複性勞損；腳踏、大屏幕和大桌子都與手腕無關。

實作建議：按上表檢查自己在家中使用電腦時的屏幕距離、椅子高度和手肘角度，找出最少一項可以改善的地方。

2.4方便用戶使用的軟件設計

人體工程學也包括軟件設計。方便用戶使用的界面應：

原則 說明
簡單清晰 版面簡潔，功能容易找到，減少用戶出錯
一致性 同一軟件以至不同平台（電腦、手機）的按鈕位置、圖示、用詞一致，用戶毋須重新學習
合適的互動設計 以清晰的提示和回饋配合用戶的使用習慣，例如以下拉式選單代替自由輸入
無障礙設計 所有人都能使用，例如可放大字體、高對比度、支援屏幕閱讀器、影片附字幕

2.5網上資訊自由的利弊

好處 壞處
可隨時取得最新資訊 大量資訊未經審查或整理，難以分辨真偽
資訊種類繁多，涵蓋各種主題 任何人都可發布，資訊來源不一定可靠，假新聞和誤導性資訊容易散播
大部分資訊免費，節省時間和金錢 可能接觸仇恨言論、極端或色情等有害內容
任何人都可以分享知識和意見 容易令人沉迷，花太多時間上網

⚠ 〔2023 卷一乙 1(d)(i)〕彼得在互聯網上找到一些資料用於比賽專題。寫出兩個驗證這些資料的方法。

✔ 與不同來源的資料互相比對；查看資料是否來自具權威的來源，例如政府或學術機構；查看資料的發布日期，確認是否過時。

✘ 只寫「再上網搜尋」而沒有說明怎樣比對，答案不夠具體。

2.6數碼隔閡與公平存取

數碼公平存取指所有人和社群都能取得並有效運用參與社會所需的數碼科技。能夠取得和有效使用資訊及通訊科技的人，與不能取得或使用的人之間的差距，稱為數碼隔閡。

成因 例子
收入 低收入家庭無法負擔電腦、智能電話或寬頻服務
年齡 長者不熟悉新科技
性別 在某些社會，女性學習資訊及通訊科技的機會較少；「科技工作較適合男性」的刻板印象
教育程度 教育程度較低的人較難有效使用網上資訊和服務
殘疾 缺乏合適的設備、軟件或無障礙網站
種族及語言 少數族裔面對語言障礙；不懂英文的人可用的網上資訊較少
地理位置 偏遠或鄉郊地區的網絡基礎設施薄弱，網速慢或不穩定

全球數碼隔閡指已發展國家和發展中國家之間在科技和互聯網普及程度上的差距。

收窄數碼隔閡的措施：在公共地方提供免費 Wi-Fi（例如「Wi-Fi.HK」）；在公共圖書館提供免費電腦；捐贈及翻新舊電腦給有需要的家庭；為長者和基層人士提供免費的資訊科技培訓；資助低收入家庭購買設備和上網服務；鼓勵女性修讀資訊及通訊科技；發展輔助技術。

數碼共融指確保所有人都能接觸和使用資訊及通訊科技並參與社會。基本條件包括：價錢實惠及可靠的上網服務、符合用戶需要的設備、使用互聯網和智能設備的培訓、高質素的技術支援，以及讓用戶能自給自足地參與的數碼環境。

⚠ 〔2023 卷一甲第 37 題〕以下哪些可以幫助收窄數碼隔閡？(1) 在公共圖書館提供免費電腦 (2) 為有需要的學生提供上網服務 (3) 為有需要的學生提供網上英文課

✔ 只有 (1) 和 (2)：兩者都令有需要的人可以接觸科技。

✘ 選 (3)：沒有電腦或上網服務的學生根本無法上網上課，網上課程不能收窄數碼隔閡。

〔改編自 2021 卷一甲第 38 題〕學校推行「自攜設備」（BYOD）讓不同背景的學生都能在課堂內外使用電子學習資源，有助收窄數碼隔閡；在餐廳設置自助點餐機、在商店推廣電子付款只是提高效率和便利，不能收窄數碼隔閡。

〔改編自 2026 模擬試卷卷一甲第 39 題〕要促進青少年的公平存取，最有效的是提供免費資訊科技課程並配備所需設備；只為有資訊科技技能的優秀學生提供獎學金，幫助的是已有能力的人。

2.7殘疾人士與輔助技術

輔助技術幫助殘疾人士使用資訊及通訊科技，是促進數碼共融的重要一環。

對象 輔助技術
視障人士 屏幕閱讀器（文字轉語音）、屏幕放大器、點字顯示器、圖像的語音描述、聲控操作
聽障人士 字幕、即時轉錄（語音轉文字）、文字訊息、以震動或閃燈代替聲音提示
肢體傷殘人士 聲控操作、眼球追蹤、經改裝的鍵盤和滑鼠、智能家居系統

網站設計亦應配合輔助技術，例如為圖像加上替代文字、讓屏幕閱讀器正確讀出內容，並選用色盲人士也能分辨的顏色。

2.8使用資訊及通訊科技的道德考慮

道德是判斷對與錯的準則；應用於使用電腦和科技時，稱為電腦道德。

議題 例子及考慮
公共安全與私隱 DNA 鑑定分析有助破案，但儲存個人 DNA 資料可能侵犯私隱，應只限司法用途並由認可化驗所處理；智能身份證的晶片資料要加密保護，以防身份被盜用；容貌識別可用於大規模追蹤，涉及數據由誰擁有、怎樣儲存和分享等問題
環境 電腦部件含鉛、汞等有毒物質，難以回收；頻繁更換仍可使用的設備製造大量電子廢物；數據中心和伺服器持續運作，耗用大量電力
社交媒體 網絡欺凌：利用互聯網騷擾或傷害他人；起底：未經同意披露他人的個人資料，以騷擾或威嚇對方，在香港屬刑事罪行；假新聞和深度偽造內容：誤導公眾、操控輿論
收集個人資料 機構收集個人資料後要妥善保護，只用於收集時說明的用途

做個負責任的數碼公民：不發布猥褻或冒犯他人的內容；不散播電腦病毒或惡意程式；未經授權不存取他人的電腦、數據或網絡；尊重其他用戶，不騷擾或威嚇他人。在香港，有犯罪或不誠實意圖而取用電腦，屬《刑事罪行條例》（第 200 章）第 161 條所訂的罪行，最高可判監禁 5 年。

⚠ 〔2023 卷一甲第 35 題〕使用社交媒體平台時，以下哪些是適當的措施？(1) 管理私隱設定，控制誰可以看到自己的帖文 (2) 使用高強度的登入密碼並定期更換 (3) 留意自己提供了哪些個人資料

✔ (1)、(2) 和 (3)。

✘ 漏選 (3)：在帳戶公開姓名、生日、學校等資料，會增加被起底或盜用身份的風險。

2.9自我檢測

1. 寫出電腦視覺綜合症的兩個症狀和兩個預防方法。

症狀：眼睛乾澀、疲勞、視力模糊、頭痛（任何兩項）。預防：遵從 20-20-20 法則；使用防眩光屏幕；在光線充足的環境使用；多眨眼（任何兩項）。

2. 解釋「20-20-20 法則」。

每使用屏幕 20 分鐘，望向最少 20 英尺（約 6 米）外的景物 20 秒。

3. 嘉麗每天使用滑鼠超過 8 小時。寫出一個可能出現的健康問題，並建議一件電子產品減低這個威脅。

手腕或手指的重複性勞損。建議使用人體工程學滑鼠。

4. 兆強長時間坐着工作，患上深層靜脈血栓。解釋成因，並建議一項預防措施。

長時間坐着，腿部缺乏活動，血液滯留在腿部靜脈形成血栓。工作時應定時站立走動或伸展雙腿。

5. 寫出方便用戶使用的軟件界面的兩項設計原則。

簡單清晰；不同平台之間保持一致；無障礙設計；合適的互動設計（任何兩項）。

6. 寫出網上資訊自由的一個好處和一個壞處。

好處：可隨時取得最新資訊；資訊種類多；大部分免費（任何一項）。壞處：資訊未經審查；來源不可靠，假新聞容易散播；容易沉迷（任何一項）。

7. 除家庭收入外，寫出兩個造成數碼隔閡的因素。

年齡、性別、教育程度、殘疾、種族及語言、地理位置（任何兩項）。

8. 非政府機構為長者提供免費的智能電話培訓班。這項計劃的主要目的是甚麼？

促進數碼共融：幫助長者掌握數碼技能，能使用網上服務，從而收窄數碼隔閡。

9. 嘉麗有聽力障礙，發現很多網上影片沒有輔助功能。建議一項輔助技術。

為影片加上字幕；或提供即時轉錄（語音轉文字）。

10. 志豪把同學的個人資料和醜聞張貼到社交媒體，讓其他人取笑。你同意他的做法嗎？為甚麼？

不同意。這是起底和網絡欺凌：未經同意披露他人的個人資料以騷擾對方，會對受害人造成傷害，在香港屬刑事罪行。

11. 寫出使用資訊及通訊科技引致的兩個環境問題。

部分電腦部件難以回收；部件含鉛、汞等有毒物質；頻繁更換設備製造大量電子廢物；數據中心耗用大量電力（任何兩項）。

12. 某公司推出免費的社交媒體應用程式並收集用戶的個人資料。寫出該公司處理這些資料時要注意的一個道德問題。

要妥善儲存及保護資料，防止外洩；或只可把資料用於向用戶說明的用途，不可挪作他用。

2.1Curriculum requirements

Identify health hazards associated with the use of ICT and propose good ergonomic practices (including reducing repetitive strain injury, suitable design and placement of furniture, software design with user-friendly features such as ease of use and a consistent user interface, and the work environment); realise the importance of equity of access, state the pros and cons of freedom of information on the Internet, and know the equity issues of the digital divide, gender equity and access for the disabled from local and global perspectives; discuss the ethical considerations on the use of ICT.

2.2Health hazards of using ICT

Health problem Symptoms Causes Prevention
Computer vision syndrome (CVS) Dry, tired, stinging eyes, blurred vision, sensitivity to glare, headaches Looking at a screen at close range for long periods, blinking less, screen reflections or glare, poor lighting Follow the 20-20-20 rule; use an anti-glare screen; use devices in well-lit surroundings; blink more; limit screen time
Back and neck pain Pain in the lower back, neck and shoulders Poor posture, staying in one position for a long time, looking down at a phone Keep a correct sitting posture; set the screen at a suitable height; stretch every 15 to 20 minutes
Deep vein thrombosis Swelling and pain in the legs Sitting for long periods without leg movement, so blood pools in the leg veins Stand up and walk around, or stretch the legs, regularly
Repetitive strain injury (RSI) Pain and swelling in the wrists, fingers and elbows, reduced mobility Repeating the same movement for long periods, e.g. typing, using a mouse, swiping a phone screen Use ergonomic keyboards and mice and wrist rests; take regular breaks and stretch the hands
Internet addiction Anxiety, depression, insomnia, social isolation, neglecting studies and responsibilities Uncontrolled, prolonged use of the Internet, video games or social media Record and limit Internet time; take part in more sports and group activities; seek help from social workers or counsellors when needed

The 20-20-20 rule: every 20 minutes of screen use, look at something at least 20 feet (about 6 metres) away for 20 seconds.

⚠ Computer vision syndrome comes from looking at a screen for long periods, not from electromagnetic radiation emitted by the monitor. In answers, write "looking at the screen for long periods", "blinking less" or "screen glare".

[adapted from 2022 P1A Q37] Spending excessive time browsing social media and watching online videos may cause eye discomfort and blurred vision, as well as alienation from family and friends; it does not improve physical fitness.

2.3Good ergonomic practices

Ergonomics studies how to design equipment, furniture, software and the work environment so that users are comfortable and safe, and injuries are reduced.

Correct posture and setup

Item Recommendation
Screen About 50 to 70 cm from the eyes; top of the screen at about eye level, with its centre about 20° below eye level; adjustable screen angle to avoid reflections
Keyboard and mouse At elbow height; elbows at about 90°, forearms and hands kept straight
Chair Adjustable height and backrest; a backrest that supports the lower back; adjustable armrests to support the forearms; a five-legged base for stability
Feet Flat on the floor; use a footrest if the feet do not reach the floor
Table Suitable height, preferably adjustable; enough space for the keyboard and mouse
Work environment Adequate lighting, with no lamp or window light shining directly on the screen; good ventilation

Ergonomic equipment: ergonomic keyboards (keeping the hands in a natural position), ergonomic mice, gel wrist rests, height-adjustable monitor stands, anti-glare screens, adjustable chairs and tables, footrests.

⚠ [2024 P1B 2(d)] Tom spends a lot of time using a keyboard in his work. Give a potential health hazard of this, and state a corresponding measure to protect his health, other than taking regular breaks.

✔ Hazard: repetitive strain injury to the wrists, carpal tunnel syndrome, or wrist, hand or finger pain. Measure: use a keyboard with an ergonomic design that keeps the hands in a natural position; or use a silicone wrist rest.

✘ Writing only "headache" or "pain" without naming the part of the body got no marks; "stop working for a while" was not accepted, as the question excluded regular breaks.

[2024 P1A Q39] From the computer use shown in the question, the ergonomic improvement needed is installing armrests on the chair, not installing a trackball or a touch screen or using a smaller table. 93% of candidates answered correctly.

[Sample Paper Section A Q40] In good ergonomic settings, the angle of the display unit and the height of the table should be adjustable; the angle of the armrest is not necessary.

[2012 Practice Paper P1A Q38] An armrest helps minimise repetitive strain injury to the fingers and wrists during typing; a footrest, a large screen and a large desk have nothing to do with the wrists.

Practical suggestion: using the table above, check the screen distance, chair height and elbow angle when you use a computer at home, and find at least one thing to improve.

2.4User-friendly software design

Ergonomics also covers software design. A user-friendly interface should be:

Principle Explanation
Simple and clear Uncluttered layout, functions easy to find, fewer user errors
Consistent Button positions, icons and wording are the same within the software and across platforms (computer, phone), so users need not relearn
Suitably interactive Clear prompts and feedback that fit users' habits, e.g. drop-down menus instead of free typing
Accessible Usable by everyone, e.g. enlargeable text, high contrast, screen reader support, captions on videos

2.5Pros and cons of freedom of information on the Internet

Pros Cons
Up-to-date information is available at any time Much information is unverified or disorganised, so truth is hard to tell from falsehood
A huge variety of information on every subject Anyone can publish, so sources may be unreliable and fake news and misleading information spread easily
Most information is free, saving time and money Users may come across harmful content such as hate speech, extremist or pornographic material
Anyone can share knowledge and opinions It is easy to become addicted and spend too much time online

⚠ [2023 P1B 1(d)(i)] Peter has found some information on the Internet for a competition project. Suggest two ways of validating the information.

✔ Cross-check with information from different sources; check whether it comes from an authoritative source, such as a government or academic institution; check the date of publication to see whether it is out of date.

✘ Writing only "search the Internet again" without saying how to cross-check is not specific enough.

2.6The digital divide and equity of access

Digital equity of access means that all individuals and communities can obtain and effectively use the digital technologies needed to take part in society. The gap between people who can obtain and effectively use ICT and those who cannot is called the digital divide.

Cause Examples
Income Low-income families cannot afford computers, smartphones or broadband
Age Elderly people are unfamiliar with new technologies
Gender In some societies women have fewer opportunities to learn ICT; the stereotype that "technology jobs suit men better"
Education level People with less education find it harder to use online information and services effectively
Disability Lack of suitable devices, software or accessible websites
Ethnicity and language Ethnic minorities face language barriers; people who do not know English have less online information available
Geographical location Weak network infrastructure in remote or rural areas, with slow or unstable connections

The global digital divide is the gap in access to technology and the Internet between developed and developing countries.

Measures to bridge the digital divide: free Wi-Fi in public places (e.g. "Wi-Fi.HK"); free computers in public libraries; donating and refurbishing old computers for families in need; free IT training for the elderly and low-income people; subsidies for low-income families to buy devices and Internet services; encouraging women to study ICT; developing assistive technologies.

Digital inclusion means ensuring that everyone can access and use ICT and take part in society. Its basic elements include affordable and reliable Internet services, devices that meet users' needs, training in using the Internet and smart devices, high-quality technical support, and a digital environment in which users can take part self-sufficiently.

⚠ [2023 P1A Q37] Which of the following can help bridge the digital divide? (1) Providing free access to computers in public libraries (2) Providing Internet access for students in need (3) Providing online English lessons for students in need

✔ (1) and (2) only: both give people in need access to technology.

✘ Choosing (3): students without computers or Internet access cannot attend online lessons at all, so online lessons do not bridge the digital divide.

[adapted from 2021 P1A Q38] A school "Bring Your Own Device" (BYOD) scheme lets students from different backgrounds use e-learning resources in and out of class, helping to narrow the digital divide; self-service ordering kiosks in restaurants and promoting electronic payment in shops only improve efficiency and convenience and do not narrow the digital divide.

[adapted from 2026 Mock P1A Q39] To promote equitable access for youth, the most effective measure is offering free IT lessons with the necessary equipment; scholarships only for outstanding students with IT skills help those who are already able.

2.7People with disabilities and assistive technologies

Assistive technologies help people with disabilities use ICT and are an important part of digital inclusion.

Users Assistive technologies
Visually impaired Screen readers (text-to-speech), screen magnifiers, braille displays, audio descriptions of images, voice control
Hearing impaired Captions, live transcription (speech-to-text), text messages, vibration or flashing lights instead of sound alerts
Physically disabled Voice control, eye tracking, adapted keyboards and mice, smart home systems

Website design should also work with assistive technologies, e.g. adding alternative text to images, letting screen readers read the content correctly, and choosing colours that colour-blind people can distinguish.

2.8Ethical considerations in using ICT

Ethics are the standards for judging right and wrong; applied to the use of computers and technology, they are called computer ethics.

Issue Examples and considerations
Public safety and privacy DNA profiling helps solve crimes, but storing personal DNA data may infringe privacy, so it should be limited to judicial use and handled by approved laboratories; the chip data of smart identity cards must be encrypted to prevent identity theft; facial recognition can be used for mass tracking, raising questions about who owns the data and how it is stored and shared
Environment Computer parts contain toxic substances such as lead and mercury and are hard to recycle; frequently replacing devices that still work produces large amounts of e-waste; data centres and servers run continuously and use large amounts of electricity
Social media Cyberbullying: harassing or harming others through the Internet; doxxing: disclosing someone's personal data without consent to harass or intimidate them, a criminal offence in Hong Kong; fake news and deepfake content: misleading the public and manipulating opinion
Collecting personal data Organisations must protect the personal data they collect and use it only for the purposes stated when collecting it

Being a responsible digital citizen: do not post obscene or offensive content; do not spread computer viruses or malicious programs; do not access others' computers, data or networks without authorisation; respect other users and do not harass or threaten anyone. In Hong Kong, access to a computer with criminal or dishonest intent is an offence under section 161 of the Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 200), punishable by up to 5 years' imprisonment.

⚠ [2023 P1A Q35] Which of the following are proper measures when using social media platforms? (1) Manage the privacy settings to control who can see your posts (2) Use a strong login password and change it regularly (3) Be aware of what personal information you have provided

✔ (1), (2) and (3).

✘ Leaving out (3): making your name, birthday or school public in your account increases the risk of doxxing or identity theft.

2.9Self-test

1. Give two symptoms of computer vision syndrome and two ways to prevent it.

Symptoms: dry eyes, tired eyes, blurred vision, headaches (any two). Prevention: follow the 20-20-20 rule; use an anti-glare screen; use devices in well-lit surroundings; blink more (any two).

2. Explain the "20-20-20 rule".

Every 20 minutes of screen use, look at something at least 20 feet (about 6 metres) away for 20 seconds.

3. Carrie uses a mouse for more than 8 hours a day. Give one possible health problem and suggest an electronic product to reduce this hazard.

Repetitive strain injury to the wrist or fingers. Suggest an ergonomic mouse.

4. Siu Keung works sitting for long periods and develops deep vein thrombosis. Explain the cause and suggest one preventive measure.

Sitting for long periods without leg movement lets blood pool in the leg veins and form clots. He should stand up and walk around, or stretch his legs, regularly while working.

5. Give two design principles of a user-friendly software interface.

Simple and clear; consistent across platforms; accessible design; suitable interaction design (any two).

6. Give one pro and one con of freedom of information on the Internet.

Pro: up-to-date information at any time; a variety of information; most of it free (any one). Con: information is unverified; sources are unreliable and fake news spreads easily; it is addictive (any one).

7. Other than family income, give two factors that cause the digital divide.

Age, gender, education level, disability, ethnicity and language, geographical location (any two).

8. An NGO offers free smartphone training classes for the elderly. What is the main purpose of this scheme?

To promote digital inclusion: helping the elderly master digital skills so they can use online services, thus narrowing the digital divide.

9. Carrie has a hearing impairment and finds that many online videos lack accessibility features. Suggest one assistive technology.

Add captions to the videos; or provide live transcription (speech-to-text).

10. Chi Ho posts a classmate's personal data and a scandal on social media so that others can laugh at him. Do you agree with what he did? Why?

No. This is doxxing and cyberbullying: disclosing someone's personal data without consent to harass them harms the victim, and is a criminal offence in Hong Kong.

11. Give two environmental problems caused by using ICT.

Some computer parts are hard to recycle; parts contain toxic substances such as lead and mercury; frequent replacement of devices produces large amounts of e-waste; data centres use large amounts of electricity (any two).

12. A company launches a free social media app and collects users' personal data. Give one ethical issue the company should note when handling the data.

It must store and protect the data properly to prevent leaks; or it may use the data only for the purposes stated to users and not for anything else.
